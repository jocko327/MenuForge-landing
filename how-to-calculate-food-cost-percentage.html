How to calculate food cost percentage

Food cost percentage is the share of a menu price that goes to ingredients. Divide what the plate costs to make by what you sell it for, then multiply by 100. A $4.20 burger that sells for $14 runs a 30% food cost.

Food cost % = (plate cost ÷ menu price) × 100

Step 1: Cost every ingredient on the plate

For each ingredient, work out the cost of the amount you use from the pack price you paid:

Ingredient cost = (pack cost ÷ pack size) × amount used ÷ yield %

A 10 lb case of ground beef at $52.80 is $5.28 per pound. A 6 oz patty (0.375 lb) costs $1.98. If you trim or cook an ingredient down, divide by the usable yield. Romaine with a 75% yield costs a third more per usable leaf than the head price suggests. See the yield and trim guide.

Step 3: Divide by the menu price

$4.20 ÷ $14.00 = 0.30, so the burger runs a 30% food cost. To find the price that hits a target, flip it:

Price at target = plate cost ÷ target food cost %

At a 28% target, the burger needs to sell for $4.20 ÷ 0.28 = $15.00.

What is a good food cost percentage?

Many full-service restaurants aim for roughly 28% to 35% across the menu, but the right number depends on your concept, prices, and labor. Individual dishes vary a lot: a steak may run 40% and a pasta 18%, and the menu still works if the mix is right. Watch the overall number and the dishes that sell the most.

Plate food cost vs period food cost

The math above is theoretical (what each plate should cost). Your actual food cost for a period comes from inventory and purchases:

Actual food cost % = (beginning inventory + purchases − ending inventory) ÷ food sales × 100

If actual runs well above theoretical, look at waste, over-portioning, comps, theft, or prices that changed since you last costed the menu.

Food cost percentage is only right on the day you calculate it. When the case price of beef moves, every recipe that uses it moves too. Try the free plate cost calculator or spreadsheet template, or let MenuForge recost every recipe automatically when an invoice comes in.

Questions

What is the formula for food cost percentage?

Food cost percentage equals plate cost divided by menu price, times 100. A $4.20 plate that sells for $14 is a 30% food cost.

What is a good food cost percentage for a restaurant?

Many full-service restaurants aim for roughly 28% to 35% across the menu. Individual dishes vary, so watch the overall mix and your best sellers.

How do I calculate menu price from food cost?

Divide the plate cost by your target food cost percentage. A $4.20 plate at a 28% target should sell for $15.00.

What is the difference between theoretical and actual food cost?

Theoretical food cost is what each plate should cost from the recipe. Actual food cost comes from beginning inventory plus purchases minus ending inventory, divided by food sales.
